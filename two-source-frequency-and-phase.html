Physics, equations and controls

Teaching connection: DSEPHY_TE_3B05, printed pp. 63–71. The unequal-frequency example extends the textbook's stationary interference pattern. Diagrams are newly drawn.

Model: v = 30 cm/s, A = 1 cm, λⱼ = v/fⱼ. Sources are at (−D/2, 0) and (+D/2, 0). θ₁ = 2πf₁(t − r₁/v), θ₂ = 2πf₂(t − r₂/v) − δ₀; y₁ = A cos θ₁, y₂ = A cos θ₂, y = y₁ + y₂. δ₀ is the initial source lag in radians. The current source lag is δ₀ + 2π(f₁ − f₂)t, displayed modulo one cycle. Negative total lag means S₂ leads S₁.

Rotating vectors: source phase φ₁ = 360°f₁t and φ₂ = 360°f₂t − δ₀, with δ₀ in degrees here. The dial uses the same cosine reference as the waves: 0° at the top is maximum upward displacement; phase increases anticlockwise (90° left, 180° bottom, 270° right). Equal-length teal and purple vectors show stages of oscillation, not spatial directions of source motion. The orange arc runs anticlockwise from S₂ to S₁ through the equivalent lag within one cycle; the signed accumulated lag is 360°(f₁ − f₂)t + δ₀. Coincident vectors have zero angular separation, including 360° multiples. These are source phases before propagation, distinct from the arrival phases at P.

Guides: arrival-phase lag θ₁ − θ₂ = 2πn gives matching phases; θ₁ − θ₂ = (2n + 1)π gives opposite phases. Equal frequencies give stationary antinodal and nodal lines, respectively. Different frequencies give moving instantaneous reinforcement and cancellation lines. These are not permanent nodes or antinodes. A fixed point does not remain cancelled. A momentary zero displacement alone does not identify a cancellation line.

Envelope: y = 2A cos[(θ₁ − θ₂)/2] cos[(θ₁ + θ₂)/2]. The readout 2A |cos[(θ₁ − θ₂)/2]| is a constant resultant amplitude only for equal frequencies. Otherwise it is a changing envelope. The guide pattern repeats every 1/|f₂ − f₁| seconds; the two individual wave displays need not repeat together then.

Circles and distances: solid circles are individual crests (+1 cm), dashed circles are troughs (−1 cm). Two crests give +2 cm, two troughs give −2 cm, and a crest with a trough gives zero at placement. Measurements use source centres; S₁P is measured in λ₁ and S₂P in λ₂. When the wavelengths differ, the same distance corresponds to different numbers of cycles. Source phase also matters, so path difference alone does not determine interference. Readouts are rounded; calculations are not.

Snapping: five independent options; enabled intersection types take priority over line snapping. Required guides and troughs are temporarily shown and their previous visibility restored on deselection. With only line options enabled, placement uses the nearest selected line at the current instant. With all off, P moves freely. P stays fixed during playback, restarting, stepping and time shortcuts, even when the selected line moves away. If no eligible target exists, free placement is used with a notice. The 3 cm source-marker exclusions always apply.

Controls: drag P, click the tank, or focus the diagram and use arrow keys (1 cm requests, accumulated for snapping). Physical settings reset time, preserve play/pause and selected snap options, and reapply placement at t = 0. Initial-phase presets also reset time. Comparison presets keep separation and display choices. Play runs continuously; Step advances 0.05 s and pauses; the quarter-cycle shortcut advances 1/(4f₁) s and pauses. Wave 1 crest at P finds its next crest, or the present crest. Display and speed changes preserve time.

Time graphs: both components and their sum at P's current fixed location. The window shows at least two periods of the slower source and, for unequal frequencies, at least one guide-pattern repeat time. Curves extend ahead of the cursor and scroll as time advances. They are predictions at the selected P, not a record of past P positions; there is no assumption of a common period.

Idealisation: equal component amplitudes everywhere, uniform speed, no attenuation, boundary reflections or startup transients. Vertical displacement is exaggerated. All code is embedded for offline use.
