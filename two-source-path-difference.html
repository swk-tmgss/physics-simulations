Teaching activities, model and keyboard help

1 · Reinforcement through a cycle

Select only Solid + solid (or only Dashed + dashed to start at two troughs). Pause if needed, then use Wave 1 crest at P. Advance ¼ cycle twice: crest + crest becomes trough + trough. Both reinforce. Passing through zero does not mean complete cancellation.

2 · Cancellation through a cycle

Select only Solid + dashed and press Play. The teal and purple contributions move in opposite directions while the orange particle stays at equilibrium. Neither individual wave has been destroyed.

3 · Same point, new wavelength

Choose P away from the centre line, then change λ. P stays fixed and both distances stay unchanged. The extra journey now contains a different number of wavelengths, changing the interference at P.

Model, equations and teaching reference

Reference: DSEPHY_TE_3B05, printed pp. 63–67: coherent sources, reinforcement, cancellation and path difference. All diagrams are newly drawn. This simulation links the wavefront view to the actual paths of one chosen observation point.

Model: sources S₁ and S₂ oscillate in phase. Each component has amplitude A = 1 cm everywhere; the speed is v = 30 cm/s and f = v/λ. At distance s from either source, the contribution is y(s,t) = A cos[2π(vt − s)/λ]. At P, substitute s = r₁ or r₂ and add y = y₁ + y₂. Crests are at +1 cm and troughs at −1 cm.

Path difference: Δ = |r₂ − r₁|. Each extra wavelength corresponds to one full cycle. For Δ = nλ, the contributions reach matching stages and the resultant amplitude is 2 cm. For Δ = (n + ½)λ, they reach opposite stages and cancel throughout the cycle. Here n = 0, 1, 2, … . At other differences the amplitude is between 0 and 2 cm, given by 2A |cos(πΔ/λ)|. An instantaneous zero is not enough to establish complete cancellation.

Idealisation: established sinusoidal waves, with equal amplitudes at P and no attenuation, startup front, boundary reflections or amplitude calculations for real spreading. The wave profiles are explanatory side views along the two paths, not two physical strings. The upper view is a map of the tank; the orange marker keeps its position on that map while the lower orange particle represents its vertical motion.

Snapping and controls: the three snap options are independent. With one or more selected, P snaps to the nearest enabled kind of intersection. Solid + solid gives +2 cm at placement; Dashed + dashed gives −2 cm; Solid + dashed gives zero, with either source providing the crest. Click a selected option to turn off only that option. With all three off, P moves freely. Intersections are calculated from circle geometry. P stays fixed during playback, stepping, restarting and both inspection shortcuts. Clicking or dragging chooses a new crossing at the current instant and preserves time and play/pause. Physical changes clear snapping, reset time and retain play/pause; P stays fixed except for the 3 cm source exclusion zones. The tank spans x = −120 to +120 cm and y = −30 to +150 cm; sources are at (−D/2, 0) and (+D/2, 0).

Playback: Play runs continuously. Restart returns to t = 0 without changing play/pause. Step adds 0.05 s and pauses. Wave 1 crest at P advances to its next crest (or keeps the current instant if already at a crest), then pauses. Advance ¼ cycle adds T/4 and pauses. Slow motion changes animation timing only. The measurement toggle preserves time, playback and P; it hides distance arrows, numerical path labels, the complete-wavelength rulers, the orange extra-distance bracket and the path-difference summary. The rulers count backwards from P and remain stationary while waves travel. A fractional interval too narrow for its label is identified as “Partial” beside its path heading.

Keyboard: Tab reaches each control. Enter or Space activates buttons; arrow keys and Home/End operate sliders. Focus the tank and use arrow keys to move P in 1 cm requests; snapping may move it further. All code is embedded, so this file works offline without external libraries.
