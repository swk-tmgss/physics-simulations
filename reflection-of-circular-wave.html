About the model and how to use it

This idealised shallow-water model uses v = √(gh), with g = 9.81 m/s² and depth h in metres; λ = v/f. It illustrates the textbook relationships rather than predicting every ripple-tank condition. Reflection leaves frequency, speed and wavelength unchanged.

O is a real point source, fixed 60 cm in front of a long straight barrier. I is its mirror position, 60 cm behind the barrier. Incident wavefronts are circles centred on O. Reflected wavefronts are the parts of circles centred on I that lie on the water side. Their imaginary extensions are dashed. An extension appears only after its corresponding wavefront reaches the barrier; it does not represent a real wave behind the barrier.

Real incident and reflected wavefronts are solid lines. They show separate components, not the resulting water height. Interference, decreasing amplitude, diffraction at barrier ends, transmission and reflections from other tank edges are not simulated. A single pulse has no repeating frequency or wavelength. Wavelength markers appear when two suitable wavefronts are visible.

Changing frequency, depth or wave mode restarts the sequence while preserving play/pause state. Display toggles keep the current time and physical wave pattern. Restart keeps your settings. Step pauses and advances time by 0.05 s. Playback changes viewing speed only. With the imaginary part hidden, the virtual source, imaginary arcs, backward ray extensions and image-distance guide all disappear. The optional rays are fixed geometrical guides at two example points.
