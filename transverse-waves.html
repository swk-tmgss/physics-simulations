Physics notes & equations

At equilibrium position x, a particle repeats the source motion after the delay x/v. Positive displacement is upward.

T = 1/f   ·   v = fλ   ·   τ = t − x/v
y(x,t) = A sin(2πfτ), once the wave has arrived
Δφ = 360° |x − xP| / λ   (mod 360°)

Before τ = 0, the particle is at rest. In “One oscillation” mode it also rests after τ = T. For continuous motion it keeps oscillating. The ideal start and stop introduce a sudden change in velocity at zero displacement. During oscillation, particle velocity is 2πfA cos(2πfτ): it is zero at ±A and greatest in magnitude at equilibrium. Arrow lengths use a fixed scale within each view, with a smaller scale in snapshots, and do not depend on playback speed.

Distances and displacements use simulation units (u); seconds are simulated seconds. The medium spans 1,000 u, with background particles every 10 u and extra markers at exact in-phase and antiphase positions relative to P. P can be selected in 0.5 u steps. The source is separate at x = 0. Changing wavelength and frequency independently also changes v. The motor linkage is schematic. The ideal wave leaves the display without reflection or damping.
