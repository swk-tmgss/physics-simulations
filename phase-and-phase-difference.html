Optional explanation: equations and radians

A phase angle specifies the stage of a cycle. Here we choose 0° at equilibrium moving upwards. With amplitude A = 1 cm, displacement is A sin θ. At 90° the displacement is +A; at 180° it is zero but moving downwards. A different zero reference is possible, provided it is used consistently.

One cycle = 360° = 2π radians. Half a cycle is 180° = π; a quarter is 90° = π/2. Angles that differ by complete cycles represent the same stage. Equations below use radians.

Lessons 1–2: θA = 2πt/T, θB = 2πt/T − δ; yA = A sin(2πt/T), yB = A sin(2πt/T − δ). A positive δ means B lags behind A. Both phases increase at the same rate, so their difference remains constant. The time delay is (δ/2π)T, or (lag in degrees / 360°)T.

Lesson 3: at distance s from either in-phase source, y(s,t) = A sin[2π(ft − s/λ)]. At P, use s = L₁ or L₂. Since f = v/λ, an extra distance Δ adds a delay Δ/v and a phase lag 2πΔ/λ radians = 360°Δ/λ. Thus an extra whole wavelength brings the same stage of the cycle; half a wavelength brings the opposite stage.

Addition: y = y₁ + y₂. For equal amplitudes, the resultant amplitude is 2A |cos(δ/2)|. A whole-cycle difference gives amplitude 2 cm; a half-cycle difference gives zero; a quarter-cycle difference gives √2 cm. Amplitude describes the largest displacement over a cycle, not the displacement at every instant.

Model, controls and textbook connection

Teaching connection: DSEPHY_TE_3B05, printed pp. 63–67: coherent waves, reinforcement and cancellation, and path difference. These lessons introduce phase before applying those ideas. All diagrams here are newly drawn; no textbook images are copied.

Idealisation: sinusoidal oscillations of amplitude 1 cm. Lessons 1–2 have period T = 1 s. Lesson 3 uses coherent, in-phase sources, speed v = 30 cm/s along both paths and equal component amplitudes at P, without attenuation or startup transients. The waves are already established at t = 0. The two path strips are explanatory contributions ending at one observation point; the orange particle is the actual resultant there. Displacement is exaggerated.

Controls: Play continues until paused. Restart sets time to zero while retaining play/pause. Step adds 0.05 s and pauses; Advance ¼ cycle adds T/4 and pauses. The cycle-position slider and its presets pause. Changing phase lag, wavelength or path difference resets time while preserving play/pause. Switching lessons pauses and resets time, retaining the lesson settings. Show radians and the equation disclosure do not change time. Slow motion changes animation timing only. Graphs show two periods with a repeating cursor; elapsed time continues increasing.

Keyboard: Tab reaches each control. Arrow keys change sliders; Home/End select their limits. Left/Right and Home/End navigate the lesson tabs. Enter or Space activates buttons. Values are rounded for display; all calculations retain full precision. The HTML works offline with no external libraries.
