About the model and how to use it

This is an idealised, one-dimensional longitudinal travelling wave. The source drives a medium represented by 40 particles, with equilibrium positions 10 cm apart. The displacement at equilibrium position x is the source's earlier displacement at time t − x/v, where v = 120 cm/s. Particles beyond the arriving disturbance remain at rest. Interactions pass the disturbance and energy through the medium; particles oscillate locally.

The dots represent particles in an ideal travelling-wave model, rather than individual air molecules. The medium continues beyond the right edge, so there is no reflected wave. The 9.4 cm amplitude limit keeps particles in order throughout the frequency range, including during live adjustments. The displayed dimensions and low frequencies are chosen for teaching; this is not a prediction of the speed of sound in air.

Frequency changes preserve the source's phase. Amplitude approaches its target at no more than 20 cm/s, including at startup; this short transition avoids jumps and keeps particles in order. After a setting has settled and its disturbance has arrived, every particle has the source's amplitude and frequency. Then T = 1/f and λ = v/f. During a change, different parts of the medium can still contain different earlier settings.

Shading is calculated from the current separation of neighbours: smaller than 10 cm indicates compression; greater than 10 cm indicates rarefaction. Labels identify local spacing minima and maxima. Particle arrows show instantaneous motion, not displacement or force; arrow length is scaled for visibility. At a turning point the velocity is zero. The enlarged view uses the same horizontal scale for equilibrium spacing and displacement.

Start/Pause freezes or resumes all motion. Step pauses and advances by 0.02 simulated seconds. Restart clears the source history and returns to rest, retaining settings. When paused, amplitude and frequency changes take effect on the next step or resume. Changing playback speed does not restart the wave. Playback speed affects viewing time only. All controls can be operated with a keyboard. Open this HTML file directly in a browser; no internet connection is needed.
