About the model and how to use it

This idealised shallow-water model uses v = √(gh), with g = 9.81 m/s² and depth h in metres, and λ = v/f. It illustrates textbook relationships rather than predicting every ripple-tank condition. Each region has uniform depth. The source frequency remains the same across the boundary.

The refraction direction follows sin r = (v₂/v₁) sin i. Solid teal lines are incident crest wavefronts. Solid orange lines are refracted wavefronts, or reflected wavefronts when the critical angle is reached or exceeded. Each part changes direction only after reaching the boundary. The lines remain perpendicular to their travelling directions.

For shallow-to-deep travel, ic = sin⁻¹(v₁/v₂). Above ic, no propagating refracted wave crosses into region 2: total internal reflection occurs. At ic, the limiting refracted direction is along the boundary (r = 90°); the animation shows reflection and a grazing-direction guide, with no wavefronts advancing into region 2. The region 2 speed readout is its model wave speed, even when no wave is transmitted. Frequency and wavelength there are then shown as dashes.

The total-reflection case is an extension beyond printed pp. 48–51. Partial reflection below the critical angle, amplitudes, evanescent fields, interference, finite-beam diffraction and reflections from the outer tank edges are omitted. The model displays wavefront geometry, not the resulting water height. A single pulse has no repeating frequency or wavelength.

Changing frequency, either depth, incidence angle or wave mode restarts the sequence and preserves play/pause state. Display toggles leave the simulation time unchanged. Restart keeps your settings. Step pauses and advances simulated time by 0.05 s. Playback speed changes viewing time only. Wavelength markers appear when two suitable wavefronts are visible. All controls work with a keyboard.
