Teaching activities, model and keyboard help

1 · Find suitable frequencies

Start at 0 Hz. Raise the frequency at fixed tension. Calculate the settings for one, two and three loops, then try the presets. One wavelength spans two loops.

2 · Change tension

Choose two loops. Keep the frequency fixed and change tension. Watch the pattern change. Use the new wave speed to calculate another suitable frequency. At fixed frequency, a lower wave speed gives a shorter wavelength.

3 · Follow particles

Follow the blue and orange particles. Particles in the same group move in phase; the two groups move in antiphase. Compare neighbouring loops, then alternate loops. Same phase does not require the same amplitude. Greenish-yellow nodes remain still; a moving particle passing through equilibrium is not a node.

Model: a uniform 1.00 m cord, mass per unit length 0.010 kg/m, with wave speed calculated from tension. A small left-hand driver and a fixed right-hand support maintain a finite-amplitude response. The displayed response varies smoothly with frequency. Close to a suitable frequency, the shape approaches the textbook sinusoidal pattern; at the exact frequency, ideal nodes and phase groups are shown. The small motion at the driver end is neglected in this stationary-wave approximation.

Scope: this established-wave teaching model illustrates patterns, wavelength and particle relationships. Changes ease into the new response; the initial travel of a disturbance, energy build-up and absolute amplitudes of a particular real cord are not modelled. There is no arbitrary maximum-amplitude clipping. The vertical scale is fixed.

Particle colours: blue and orange identify opposite phase groups, not positive and negative displacement. Each particle keeps its colour during a cycle. Larger greenish-yellow particles mark every exact node, including both ends. Nodes do not have an oscillation phase. Only these three particle colours are used. While the driver is off, during changes, or between exact suitable frequencies, the grey string is shown without classified particles. Choose a calculated preset and let the pattern settle to see the groups.

Controls: first choosing a positive frequency starts playback. Once you pause, further parameter changes keep it paused. Frequency and tension changes preserve elapsed time; the driver's cycle remains continuous. Restart returns to 0 Hz, time zero and a resting string, while retaining tension, slow-motion and display settings. At 0 Hz the driver is off and an existing response settles away. Cycle stepping is unavailable at 0 Hz. Display options preserve time and playback. Particles move vertically, with fixed horizontal positions during playback.

Keyboard: Tab to controls; Enter or Space activates buttons. Use arrow keys and Home/End on sliders. Escape closes open explanations.
