Displacement is actual position minus equilibrium position. Right is positive throughout. The same selected particle and colour link the physical dot, its measured displacement, its point on the distance graph and its time trace. The light vertical guides in the two upper panels have identical distance positions. Letters above the physical row mark fixed equilibrium positions; highlighted labels follow the selected particle.

Version 2 starts with every particle at equilibrium and at rest. The source begins moving only when time advances. Its vibration reaches A after 0.083 s, B after 0.167 s, and so on. Each particle’s time graph remains at zero until the disturbance arrives. With the default settings, the established wave eventually has amplitude 8 cm and wavelength 80 cm, at a frequency of 1.50 Hz and wave speed of 120 cm/s. These match the amplitude and wavelength illustrated on textbook p. 162. Displacements are shown to two decimal places. The source is drawn schematically above the particles. It is located 10 cm to the left of A's equilibrium position in the model.

At the start, all measured displacements are zero, so plotting the initial snapshot places every point on the distance axis. Start linked motion, let the wave form, then use Build a snapshot to investigate a later instant. Restart retains the chosen source settings, returns every particle and the source to equilibrium at rest at t = 0, clears the plotted points and starts a fresh time recording. Build a snapshot freezes the current instant and clears only the distance-graph measurements. Selecting a particle in this mode adds its point. Plot all particles completes the measurements; Show the curve then reveals the continuous displacement field. Starting or stepping returns to the linked live view, with all particle points visible. Pausing the live view preserves its points.

Each particle has its own displacement–time record, available even if it was not previously selected. The trace stops at the present time; no future motion is drawn. Older motion scrolls out of the selected four- or eight-second window. Switching particles, showing the comparison, changing the time window and revealing the curve do not advance or reset time. The A–B–C comparison uses three separate graphs with identical time and displacement scales and a common current-time cursor. In an established sinusoidal wave, equal-period traces are time-shifted; particles in phase can have identical traces.

The model stores the source displacement and evaluates s(d,t) from the source at t − (d + 10)/120. Its history before t = 0 is zero. The source starts at zero phase and its amplitude increases smoothly from zero at no more than 20 cm/s. Subsequent frequency changes preserve phase, and amplitude changes use the same rate limit. The 9.4 cm amplitude limit keeps the displacement field ordered at all allowed frequencies and during adjustments. Source changes reach each location after its own delay. Every displayed value and graph is calculated from this same history. The curve between measured points is the model's continuous field, not straight lines between particles. There are no reflections, attenuation or random molecular motions.

Use the labelled selector if particles are too close to tap individually. Buttons, selectors and sliders work with a keyboard. This file works offline without external libraries or internet access.
